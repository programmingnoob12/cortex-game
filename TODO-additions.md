## 27. Marketing hype animations

Short, loud, loopable animations built for social rather than for the app:
punchy motion, big numbers climbing, hard cuts, a repeatable visual signature
people recognise on sight. The reference is meme-coin launch marketing, where
the whole point is momentum and repetition rather than explaining the product.

Candidates to animate:
- A gem climbing tiers, tier name slamming in on each step
- A grid lighting up faster and faster as N climbs, ending on a record
- Score line ripping upward across a chart with the number counting up
- The record reveal already in the app, cut down to 6 seconds

Deliverables: a set of square and vertical exports, no audio dependency, plus
one with the celebration track.

## 29. Pre-checkout quiz showing cognitive indices rising

Modelled on Chessreps' pre-checkout flow. A short quiz sits between the app
and the checkout, and its result screen shows projected gains across named
indices, animated upward:

- Working Memory Index
- Fluid Reasoning Index
- Processing Speed
- Full Scale IQ estimate

Open question to settle before building: how the numbers are derived. Anything
presented as an IQ score needs to be either genuinely computed from the user's
own session data or clearly labelled as a projection, otherwise it is a
straightforward false claim about a health-adjacent measure and a real legal
risk. The honest version is at least as persuasive: show their actual measured
progress on the exercises, and label the index figures as estimates.

This supersedes the earlier item 21 (quiz before checkout), which had no
detail.

## 30. Ragebait for marketing, occasionally

Deliberately provocative posts as part of the marketing mix, not the whole of
it. Works by drawing argument in the replies, which is engagement either way.

Angles that fit the product:
- Claims about which training actually transfers, stated flatly
- Rankings that put a popular app or method last
- A hard take on IQ, talent or "you cannot train intelligence"
- Posting a score and calling a threshold average

Worth deciding up front: keep it to opinions about the field, not attacks on
named people or groups. The first kind gets argued with and spreads; the
second gets the account reported and follows the brand around afterwards.
Ration it too. An account that is only ragebait stops converting, because
nobody trusts it enough to buy.
